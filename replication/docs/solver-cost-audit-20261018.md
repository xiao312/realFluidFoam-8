## Measured section costs

| Replay | Seconds/step | Chemistry | Pairwise diffusion correction | Thermophysical work | Pressure |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One-step, 1 atm, 120 ms | 0.265 | 5.7% | 6.0% | 78.7% | 1.90% |
| GRI30, 1 atm, 1 ms | 8.123 | 24.8% | 20.6% | 52.0% | 0.07% |
| GRI30, 200 atm, initialized | 9.253 | 22.8% | 18.2% | 55.4% | 0.08% |
| GRI30, air 100 atm, initialized | 9.337 | 22.8% | 18.6% | 55.9% | 0.12% |
| Premixed phi=1.10, 80 microseconds | 3.097 | 47.4% | 14.2% | 36.4% | 0.05% |

Thermophysical work here means `thermo.correct()` plus the four separate Cp, Cv,
kappa and W queries. In the 1 atm GRI30 case those parts cost 23.5% and 28.4%
respectively. Species linear solves themselves cost about 0.15%; pressure is not
the measured bottleneck. Remaining time covers other assembly, energy diffusion,
flow work and bookkeeping. Fractions are rounded and are wall-time shares, not
exclusive CPU samples.

GRI30 counterflow is 31–35 times slower per timestep than this one-step replay.
Premixed timesteps cost less but advance only 1e-8 s, versus 1e-6 s in counterflow.
At 3.097 s/step, a 1 ms premixed interval requires about 86 wall-hours at four
ranks if throughput stays fixed. The 70 ms GRI30 targets require about 158–182
wall-hours at eight ranks. These estimates omit queue delays and future changes
in stiffness; endpoint duration is not a convergence criterion.

The optimization priority differs by family: repeated mixture/transport work and
diffusion assembly are the majority in counterflow; chemistry is the largest
single section in premixed, with thermophysical work still substantial. Changing
the pressure linear solver would address much less than one percent here.

## Measurement design

Five timesteps are replayed for five states: original-input one-step at 120 ms,
1 atm GRI30 at 1 ms, the initialized 200 atm and air GRI30 cases at zero stage time,
and phi=1.10 premixed at 80 microseconds. The same eight/four-rank decompositions,
qualified libraries and numerical settings are retained. A forced write occurs
at the final step. Section summaries use steps 2–4, excluding restart setup,
first-step adaptive chemistry initialization and the forced checkpoint write.

Timers record steady-clock elapsed time independently on every rank without added
barriers. They include MPI waits inside the timed section. Nested timers overlap:
chemistry and diffusion corrections are children of species_total; thermo_correct
is a child of energy_total. Percentages use the mean rank timestep duration and
must not sum parents and children. These are state-specific, short replay timings,
not whole-campaign averages or a strong-scaling benchmark.

Equal wall durations across ranks cannot rule out chemistry load imbalance:
collective operations may synchronize ranks inside an enclosing section. These
timers do not separate useful CPU work from MPI spin/wait time.

The 1 atm GRI30 and premixed cases also run with the original executable from the
same checkpoint. Final field bodies are compared after removing location-bearing
FoamFile headers. That checks instrumentation equivalence, not agreement with an
uninterrupted production trajectory. Raw data remain under `jobs/124765060`.

## Algorithm and specific costs

Source paths below are relative to the pinned upstream revision.

1. `applications/solvers/realFluidReactingFoam/YEqn.H` calls
   `reaction->correct()` once per outer iteration. The selected laminar chemistry
   ultimately invokes `SRKchungTakaStandardChemistryModel::solve`. It loops through
   local cells, converts mass fractions to concentrations, and integrates each
   reacting cell until its requested chemistry interval is covered.
   GRI30 has 53 species; the ODE state also contains T and p (55 components).
   Pressure has zero time derivative in this chemistry system. SEULEX uses
   Jacobians, dense LU decomposition/back-substitution, extrapolation and adaptive
   internal steps. Its actual cost depends on local composition, temperature and
   tolerances, not just cell count. The counterflow relative tolerance is 0.1;
   premixed uses 1e-6. Both use absolute tolerance 1e-8.

2. The same species header nests a complete species loop inside another species
   loop to compute two explicit diffusion-correction Laplacians. With 53 species
   this is 2 x 53 x 53 = 5,618 full-field Laplacian evaluations per outer iteration,
   before the species equation solves. These operations construct temporary
   fields and perform boundary/processor communication. The correction runs even
   for the inert species because its active-species test follows the inner loop.
   By contrast, a five-species one-step mechanism makes 50 such calls. Solving
   most species equations in one or two linear iterations does not make these
   assembly and correction costs disappear.

3. `EEqn.H` adds species enthalpy diffusion with one Laplacian and one divergence
   per species, solves enthalpy, and calls `thermo.correct()`.
   `src/thermophysicalModels/basic/psiThermo/hePsiThermo.C::calculate` assembles
   each cell/boundary mixture, inverts enthalpy to temperature, evaluates psi,
   viscosity and thermal diffusivity, then evaluates all species diffusivities.
   `chungTakaTransport::Dimix(i,p,T)` loops over the other species and evaluates
   Fuller/Takahashi binary transport, giving another quadratic species operation.
   The section timer covers this whole correction; it does not separately measure
   Newton iterations, EOS evaluation, binary transport and allocation.

4. At the end of every timestep, `realFluidReactingFoam.C` requests Cp, Cv, kappa
   and W as separate full fields. The generic `heThermo::volScalarFieldProperty`
   calls `cellMixture()` for every cell and `patchFaceMixture()` for every face.
   `SRKchungTakaMixture` recomputes mole fractions, quadratic EOS/Chung mixture
   sums, and composition-dependent pair tables TCMD/PCMD. `updateTRANS` copies
   these lists and the MMD/SIGMD pair tables into the mutable mixture object.
   This work is repeated even for the molecular-weight query. Non-empty boundary
   field entries, including wedge and processor faces, also incur evaluations.
   The end-of-step Dimix and hei getters return cached fields; copying these is
   distinct from computing diffusion coefficients in `thermo.correct()`.

5. Momentum and two pressure corrections solve the flow coupling. Pressure
   assembly includes density/flux correction and MPI reductions. The late
   production logs show roughly 4+18 pressure iterations for 1 atm GRI30 and
   1+5 for premixed, versus mostly one species/enthalpy iteration. Iteration counts
   alone cannot rank cost against the chemistry and species-pair operations.

## Additional correctness observation

`heThermo.C` fills `heList_` in its two constructors; `hei()` returns that stored
field. Searching the basic-thermo source finds no later writes to `heList_`.
`hePsiThermo::correct()` calls `calculate()`, which refreshes mixture properties
and diffusivities but does not refresh this species-enthalpy cache. Therefore
the end-of-step assignment `hei[i] = thermo.hei(i)` does not by itself recompute
species enthalpies at the current T and p. This deserves a separate correctness
and restart-consistency test before changing it; no correction is made here.
It also limits comparisons between restarted and uninterrupted trajectories.

## Optimization boundaries

Measure section shares before choosing an optimization. Possible follow-ups are
reusing composition-dependent mixture data across property queries, reducing
pair-table allocations/copies, and separating EOS-only queries from transport
setup. Cache invalidation must follow species changes and must preserve boundary
behavior. Diffusion correction optimization must preserve the face discretization
and sequential species-update semantics; replacing sums of discrete Laplacians
with a continuum identity is not automatically equivalent. Chemistry tolerances,
mechanism reduction and larger flow timesteps change numerical/model accuracy and
need separate validation. More MPI ranks require an actual scaling test.
